PILLA

Parametric Information Layering Logic
Anatemnein — the tablet that writes upon itself
© 1993–2026 Abhishek Choudhary. All rights reserved.

Abstract

Modern pharmaceutical tablets communicate remarkably little information despite being highly engineered objects. A typical tablet conveys identity through colour, size, shape, score lines and embossed text — channels constrained by surface area, human readability, manufacturing limits and regulatory convention.

This work proposes PILLA (Parametric Information Layering Logic — Anatemnein): a geometric framework in which the manufactured surface of a tablet becomes a recursively addressable information substrate. Rather than a mere dosage form, the tablet is modelled as a stratified engineering boundary that simultaneously supports structural function, human interpretation, machine-readable identification and multiscale geometric encoding.

The framework unifies differential geometry, boundary representations, feature-based manufacturing and information theory into a single engineering architecture for pharmaceutical surfaces. This is presented as a proposed engineering framework — established components synthesised into a new architecture — not a finished mathematical theory.

1 — The Problem

Medicines still rely on primitive identification. A tablet typically communicates only colour, approximate shape, embossed text, one or two score lines and manufacturer markings.

Yet the tablet already possesses a highly engineered three-dimensional boundary. Almost all of that geometric surface is unused as an information channel — faces, edges, feature boundaries, curvature and hierarchical geometry are treated as manufacturing artefacts rather than carriers.

Current channelApprox. capacityBinding constraint
Colour~10 bitsHuman distinguishability, colour blindness

Total current capacity ≈ 100–150 bits of reliable, human-readable information per tablet.

2 — The Opportunity

Every manufactured tablet already contains faces, edges, feature boundaries, curvature, tolerances and hierarchical geometry. Instead of treating these as artefacts, PILLA makes them an information architecture. Geometry itself becomes communication.

Key insight: the engineered boundary ∂T is not a constraint — it is the medium. Information is not stored in a tablet; the tablet's geometry is the information.

Leveraging the whole surface opens: multiscale geometry (faces → grooves → microfacets → nanotexture), topological features (face count, connectivity), curvature signatures, colour modulation (hue, saturation, brightness, fluorescence), surface texture and feature composition (grooves, bevels, facets, pits, ridges, embossing).

Estimated capacity: 1,000–10,000+ bits per tablet across all scales and modalities, bounded by manufacturing resolution and observational capability.

3 — Multi-Layer Communication Architecture

Information exists simultaneously at several layers, each addressing a different observer and scale. These are recursive geometric layers, not software layers — different audiences read different resolutions of the same surface.

Layer 1 — Human recognition (macroscopic). Colour, shape, size, logo, embossed name. Audience: patients, caregivers. Purpose: recognition, compliance. Timescale: seconds.
Layer 2 — Clinical recognition (mesoscale). Score lines, groove orientation/count, bevel profile, subdivision marks. Audience: doctors, pharmacists, nurses. Purpose: strength verification, subdivision guidance. Timescale: 5–10 s.
Layer 3 — Optical machine recognition (microscale). Structured micro-grooves, geometric glyphs, polygonal pits, orientation codes. Audience: pharmacy automation, hospital scanners, phones. Purpose: identification, authentication, inventory. Timescale: ms.
Layer 4 — High-density encoding (nanoscale). Recursive surface partition, micro-facets, nested grooves, feature topology. Audience: regulators, forensics. Purpose: batch, lot, expiry, formulation, anti-counterfeiting.
Layer 5 — Manufacturing layer (subsurface). Material composition, compression signature, die-wear fingerprint. Audience: QA, forensics. Detection: spectral analysis, metallography.
Macroscopic (cm) → Patient Mesoscale (mm) → Clinician Microscale (μm) → Machine Nanoscale (nm) → Forensics Not software strata — the SAME object read at DIFFERENT magnification. Like a map: each zoom reveals different information on one surface.

Groove kernel

G = (Γ, L, R, S) Γ cross-sectional profile (V, U, trapezoidal, spline, polygonal) L sweep trajectory R orientation S optional scaling along the sweep

A groove encoding profile (3 bits) + orientation (4) + position (4) + depth (3) = 14 bits. A tablet with 4–6 grooves carries 56–84 bits of pure geometry.

6 — Encoding, Address & Grammar

Recursive address

Tablet 1 → Face 1.2 → Groove 1.2.1 → Left wall 1.2.1.L → Microfacet 1.2.1.L.4

Every geometric entity is uniquely addressable within the recursive decomposition (Axiom of Addressability).

Feature calculus (non-commutative)

G(B(T)) ≠ B(G(T)) grooving a bevelled tablet ≠ bevelling a grooved tablet → feature operators form an operator calculus, generally non-commutative

Feature grammar

Σ = { bevel, pit, ridge, facet, groove, colour, texture } (alphabet) words = feature sequences sentences = manufactured tablets language = manufacturing

Error-correcting geometry

QR codes use Reed–Solomon. Geometry needs redundancy too: if three grooves encode one symbol and one groove abrades, the symbol should still recover. Wear makes capacity time-dependent — dX/dt = W(X, E).

7 — Machine Decoding

Image → Lens correction → Segmentation → Pose estimation → 3D reconstruction → Surface unwrap → Feature graph → Recursive parser → Decoder → Information

Unlike a QR code, the geometry is inseparable from the object — it cannot be photocopied onto a counterfeit. Depth is recovered via structured light, stereo, photometric stereo or time-of-flight to reconstruct microgeometry.

8 — Clinical Advantages

Patients: large visible markings, colour-guided dosing, reduced confusion, better compliance.

Doctors: immediate identification, strength verification, subdivision guidance.

Pharmacists: automated dispensing, inventory, authentication, verification.

Hospitals: closed-loop administration, electronic verification, fewer medication errors.

Manufacturers: traceability, counterfeit resistance, quality control, production analytics.

Regulators: inspection, auditability, recall management, supply-chain transparency.

11 — Critique of the Existing Posters

The AI-generated poster misread the theory. Each conceptual error is marked below.

❌ 1 — "Layer" misread. Shown as software/OSI strata. In PILLA layers are recursive geometric layers on one boundary; patient/doctor/machine are audiences at different magnifications, not stacked channels.
❌ 2 — Information floating in space. Poster: information → layer → machine. PILLA: geometry is the information — it must live on faces, not hover beside them.
❌ 3 — Colour as decoration. Colour is a parametric encoding dimension attached to specific faces (top = class, floor = dose, walls = timing).
❌ 4 — Grooves nearly absent. Grooves are the central mechanism (G = Γ,L,R,S), not a minor detail.
❌ 5 — Face algebra gone. No top/bottom/rim, no bevel/chamfer/groove/facet, no gross-vs-net face creation.
❌ 6 — Recursion shown as exploded disks. It should be a zoom (map-like), not separated components.
❌ 7 — Mathematical hierarchy missing. Topology → geometry → features → recursion → encoding → manufacturing → observation → decoding.
❌ 8 — Geometry demoted. Everything looks like software; geometry must be primary — it survives wetting, wear and crushing.
❌ 9 — Human/machine as parallel channels. They are one geometry at different resolutions.
❌ 10 — No pitch / capacity. N_s ≈ A/p², I ≈ N_s·log₂M absent — capacity never quantified.
❌ 11 — No feature grammar. Alphabet → words → sentences (tablets) → language (manufacturing).
❌ 12 — Logo not explained. The mark shows the tablet writing — but never what it writes (onto its own surface).
❌ 13 — Information not on faces. Floating labels instead of colour/geometry instantiated on named faces.
❌ 14 — Electronic/software confusion. The root error: "information layering" read as microservices/OSI. It is recursive geometric layering.
❌ 15 — No mathematical centrepiece. I = f(A,p,Φ,R,M,O) should dominate the visual.
